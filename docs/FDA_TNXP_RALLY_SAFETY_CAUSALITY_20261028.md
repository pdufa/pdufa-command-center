# TNXP RALLY — adverse-event withdrawal versus causality

Source: SEC-filed 2022-03-21 release, contemporaneous before
2025-08-15 FDA decision:
https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1430306/000138713122003807/ex99-01.htm

RALLY randomized 514 patients across 36 US sites.
- TEAEs leading to early discontinuation: 15.2% TNX-102 SL
  versus 6.2% placebo.
- Six serious adverse events in the TNX-102 SL group;
  sponsor states none was judged related to investigational product.
- Oral hypoesthesia 28.1% active vs 0.8% placebo.
- Somnolence 4.7% active vs 0.8% placebo.
- Sedation 3.9% active vs 0.8% placebo.
- Primary pain endpoint failed (p=0.115 using prespecified MMRM+MI).
- Alternative MMRM without imputation (p=0.014) is not a
  substitute for the failed primary endpoint.

**Methodological guardrail:** A treatment-emergent adverse event or
an adverse-event-related withdrawal is not equivalent to an
adjudicated adverse drug reaction. Sponsor causality classifications
must not be treated as independently validated. Distinguish event
frequency, severity, seriousness, discontinuation, relatedness,
and effect on missing-data estimands.
