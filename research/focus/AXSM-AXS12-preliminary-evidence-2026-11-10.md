# AXSM AXS-12 (reboxetine) — preliminary clinical and regulatory evidence review
Date: 2026-11-10. **NOT_ADJUDICATED; independent challenge NOT_COMPLETED; no probability generated.** Retrospective/outcome-aware evidence; not a blind prediction.

## Correct trial-to-drug mapping
- **NCT05059223:** AXS-12, SYMPHONY, Phase 3 narcolepsy/cataplexy efficacy study (protocol AXS-12-301).
- **NCT05113745:** AXS-12, ENCORE, Phase 3 long-term follow-up with **24-week open-label exposure followed by four-week randomized withdrawal** (protocol AXS-12-302). The randomized-withdrawal population is enriched by completion of prior SYMPHONY treatment; its estimand differs from the initial randomized trial. Registry: https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05059223 and https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05113745
- Do not confuse AXS-12 with **AXS-05** (dextromethorphan/bupropion), an entirely different Axsome program. AXS-05's 2026 Alzheimer agitation regulatory decision is not the PDUFA event associated with these FOCUS NCTs.

## Regulatory evidence
- **July 15, 2026:** Axsome disclosed FDA acceptance of the AXS-12 NDA for cataplexy in narcolepsy, **PDUFA goal May 1, 2027**, and that FDA did not then plan an advisory committee meeting. SEC-filed press release: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1579428/000119312526304039/axsm-ex99_1.htm
- **November 2, 2026:** Axsome 3Q update reaffirmed that AXS-12 NDA remained under review and May 1, 2027 target action. Source: https://www.axsome.com/news/axsome-therapeutics-reports-third-quarter-2026-financial-res-13311
- FDA acceptance is not approval, and absence of a planned advisory committee is not a quantitative predictor by itself.

## Preliminary clinical assessment priorities
1. Retrieve SYMPHONY's primary report, complete protocol, registered primary cataplexy endpoint, baseline attack frequency, multiplicity strategy, treatment effect and confidence intervals.
2. For ENCORE, distinguish 24-week open-label safety and symptom trajectories from the **four-week randomized-withdrawal** contrast; account for completer selection and withdrawal effects.
3. Assess sleepiness/function outcomes independently from cataplexy frequency, and verify measurement/reporting of patient-important benefit.
4. Review adverse events, cardiovascular and neuropsychiatric safety, concomitant medications and drug interactions, dose/exposure and discontinuation.
5. Compare relevant established cataplexy therapies and clinical population generalizability.
6. Obtain independent specialist challenge and source-verified FDA/CMC status before adjudication.
